2. The physics the paper relies on (its Figure 4)

The plant is held at each DO set point under steady dry-average influent for 20 simulated days. More oxygen lowers effluent ammonium but raises effluent total nitrogen, because less denitrification happens in the anoxic tanks. The two discharge limits therefore pull in opposite directions, so an optimum exists for an agent to find. Recomputed from experiments/07_figures.py's procedure.

One simulator fix was needed first. With the original influent generator the ammonium load peak was only 1.25× the mean, so effluent NH₄ never neared 4 mg/L and the constraint in both rewards never bound. The pollutograph was sharpened to the documented BSM1 statistics (influent SNH 9–44 mg/L, load peak 1.5× the mean). Under real diurnal influent the fraction of time above each limit then becomes:

5. The cost the paper does not report

The paper reports energy and mean effluent quality, but not how often the limit is broken. Each point is one run, with energy on the vertical axis (lower is cheaper) and the share of the week with effluent NH₄ above 4 mg/L on the horizontal axis (left is cleaner).

Weather

DDPG-B's saving comes from violating the limit more often. On dry weather it exceeds 4 mg/L for 38 % of the week, against 12 % for PID. That is what eq. (23) asks for: at 0.42 per mg/L of excess against a full-aeration penalty of 1.0, a long run of small exceedances costs less than the air saved. The weight acts like a constraint without being one.

6. Three corrections the published recipe needed

As published, the agent collapsed to zero aeration and stayed there. Each fix below is documented in docs/04_second_paper.md §5 and implemented in src/wwtp/rl/.

① The observation is not Markov

The action is an increment on KLa₅, so the actuator position is a hidden integrator. Once DO saturates near zero the agent cannot tell KLa₅ = 0 from KLa₅ = 20. Correlated OU exploration noise (θ = 0.15, σ = 0.2, about 7 steps of memory) pins the actuator at a rail within a few hundred steps. Fix: add KLa₅/240 to the observation, and use an absolute action.

② The decision interval is too short

At 45 s with γ = 0.99 the effective horizon is about 100 steps, or 75 min. Effluent NH₄ responds to aeration over hours, so the penalty for cutting air falls outside the horizon while the energy saving is immediate. Fix: hold each action for 20 periods (15 min).

③ The value scale stalls the critic

The critic has to learn values near −250 from rewards near −2.5. Instrumented, ∂Q/∂a has the wrong sign for the first four simulated weeks, and by then the tanh actor has saturated. Fix: scale the reward by (1 − γ), add weight decay on the actor, and penalise the pre-tanh output.

8. Our extension: PANDA-RL

Four changes to the network, each aimed at a problem found in §6. With 1 quantile, CVaR α = 1, no constraints and no forecast, PANDA-RL reduces exactly to DDPG. src/wwtp/rl/panda_rl.py

① Forecast input

The actor and critic also see the forecaster's 6-number context: predicted relative change of flow, NH₄ and COD, forecast uncertainty and P(wet). The policy becomes disturbance-scheduled, so it does not have to wait hours to see the effect of its own action. (The P(wet) component is near chance on fresh data; see Prediction models.)

② Distributional critic + CVaR

32 return quantiles with a quantile Huber loss. The actor maximises the mean of the worst 30 % of returns (CVaR at α = 0.3) instead of the expectation, because a discharge consent constrains the tail, not the average.

③ Constraints instead of guessed weights

The reward keeps only −KLa₅/240. Each discharge limit gets its own cost critic and Lagrange multiplier, updated by dual ascent on the measured violation rate. The operator states “NH₄ above 4 at most x % of the time” instead of guessing 0.38 and 0.42.

④ Twin-assisted updates (Dyna)

A learned aeration twin supplies synthetic transitions. Evaluated in §11. It buys sample efficiency but breaks the constraint, and the attempted fix did not work.

9. Energy against violation rate: the frontier

A single operating point cannot show whether floating DO saves anything, because saving and violations move together. Each PANDA-RL run below has a different ammonium budget, and its Lagrange multiplier finds the weight that delivers it. One seed each.

10. The network changes, isolated

Both arms use paper 2's own reward −(KLa₅/240 + β₂·max(S_NH,e − 4, 0)), with no Lagrange multiplier, and β₂ is swept. The arms differ only in the network. The comparison is between Pareto fronts, because at equal β₂ different arms settle at different violation rates.

Not controlled for: PANDA-fw reads 6 extra inputs and its critic has 32 outputs against 1, so it is matched to DDPG-B in neither parameters nor inputs. Part of any gap could be capacity. Every point is one seed.

11. Does the learned plant model buy sample efficiency?

Every arm gets the same number of real interactions. Only the number of synthetic batches per real batch changes.

Negative result, kept. The twin does speed up learning of the energy objective, but with roughly 4× the gradient steps the Lagrange multiplier, which is stepped only at episode boundaries, cannot keep up, and violations rise from 14 % to 67 %. Stepping the dual inside the episode did not fix it (68 %). The proposed fix, scaling the dual step with the Dyna ratio, is untested.

12. Caveats

  • The plant is a re-implementation of BSM1 and the influent is statistically equivalent. Absolute numbers are not directly comparable with the paper's tables, so every claim is a comparison between methods on the same simulator.
  • The learners are seed-sensitive. Two seeds per method in the benchmark, one per point in the sweeps.
  • Convergence took about 15–20 simulated training weeks, far more than the paper's “7 days of online interactive learning”.
  • PANDA-RL at a 5 % ammonium budget does not reach it: PID itself exceeds the limit 12 % of the time, so a 5 % budget needs more air than PID and leaves no energy to save.
