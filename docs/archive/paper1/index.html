1. The plant and the control problem

BSM1 (IWA Benchmark Simulation Model no. 1) is the standard test plant in wastewater control. Reactors 1–2 are anoxic (no air): bacteria use nitrate instead of oxygen and turn it into nitrogen gas (denitrification). Reactors 3–5 are aerated: nitrifying bacteria oxidise ammonium to nitrate. The clarifier separates clean effluent from sludge, and the sludge is returned to reactor 1.

3. The algorithm, equation by equation

Each step of Algorithm 1, the equation it comes from, and the line of code that implements it.

  1. Decentralised model (eq. 4). Each subsystem i evolves as its own dynamics plus an unknown coupling from the whole state:
    s(k+1, i) = F_i( s(k, i), u(k, i) ) + G_i( s(k) )
    G_i is never identified. The utility in step 4 is how the paper copes with it.
  2. Tracking error (eq. 6). z_k = s_k − r_k, r = [1, 2]. agent.py:235
  3. Expert prior + learned term (eq. 7).
    u(k, i) = b(k, i) + a(k, i)
    b is an incremental PID with the paper's gains:
    Δb_k = Kp (e_k − e_k−1) + Ki e_k + Kd (e_k − 2 e_k−1 + e_k−2) Kp = diag{100, 100000} Ki = diag{20, 30000} Kd = diag{10, 1000}
    pid.py:50, agent.py:243, agent.py:251. The action network's outer weights start at zero (zero_outer=True, agent.py:109), so a₀ = 0 and the controller starts as the PID. That is the safety argument of the paper, and test_maacc_starts_from_the_expert_prior checks it.
  4. Incremental policy (eq. 8). The actor outputs an increment, not the action:
    Δa(k, i) = π_i( z(k, i) ) a(k, i) = a(k−1, i) + Δa(k, i)
    A disturbance then corrupts only one increment. agent.py:149–158
  5. Coupling-aware utility (eq. 10).
    U_i = W_i z_i² + Y_i Δa_i² + R_i a_i² + zᵀ P_i z ← full error vector
    The last term charges agent i for the other loop's error too, so each agent works the global objective while moving only its own actuator. agent.py:139
  6. Critic (eqs. 9, 13). Q_i(z, Δa, a) ≈ cost-to-go, a single-hidden-layer tanh net with only the outer weights β adapted online. Bellman equation: Q(x_{k−1}) = U_k + λ Q(x_k), λ = 0.9. agent.py:161–176, networks.py
  7. Critic update (eqs. 15, 18). As printed, the update diverges (see §6). It is replaced with the TD(0) direction on the same residual. agent.py:170–174
  8. Actor update (eq. 17). L_a = ½ Q²; gradient Q · ∂Q/∂Δa · ∂Δa/∂(net output) back into the actor's outer weights. agent.py:178–184
  9. Stability (Theorem 1). The weight errors are uniformly ultimately bounded (UUB) for l_c < 1/(λ²‖ϑ‖²) and l_a < 1/(‖δ‖²(β^M C)²). Closed-loop stability of the tracking error is not proved. It is inherited from the stable expert prior.

6. A flaw in eq. (15), found during reproduction

The paper's critic error is e_k = U_k + λ Q(x_k) − Q(x_{k−1}), and eq. (18) descends ½e² in the weights that produce Q(x_k), the later prediction. For slowly varying states (x_k ≈ x_{k−1}, which describes a wastewater plant) the update becomes

Q_k ← Q_{k−1} · ( 1 + l_c λ (1 − λ) ‖ϑ‖² ) − l_c λ ‖ϑ‖² U_k

The multiplier is greater than 1 for every positive learning rate, so the recursion grows without bound. The standard TD(0) direction corrects Q(x_{k−1}) toward U_k + λQ(x_k) instead, and it converges. No positive l_c makes the gain fall below 1. Theorem 1 is not contradicted, because it bounds the weight error (UUB) and a bound can be very large. The paper's condition l_c < 1/(λ²‖ϑ‖²) prevents overshoot within one step. It does not make the recursion a contraction.

7. What does the learned term do? A control experiment

An earlier version of this reproduction gave the action network a bias input and a leaky integrator (0.999·a). Its increment at zero error was then non-zero, so the learned term drifted into its clip and stayed there 92 % of the run, acting as a constant offset. Both were removed: eq. (8) is pure integration, and an incremental policy must emit nothing at zero error (now a test). To separate learning from re-tuning, two learning-free controllers run through the same harness, noise and seeds (experiments/17_maacc_offset_control.py):

  • PID + offset: the PID prior plus a constant (0, −9,223), the old MAACC with its learned term frozen at the clip.
  • PID-clamp: the PID prior with its internal Qa state allowed down to −9,223 instead of 0. The plant still clips the applied flow at 0. This is anti-windup re-tuning of the prior.

Two earlier claims were withdrawn when this benchmark ran. (1) “MAACC never reliably beats a well-tuned PID” was too strong: it was based on spot checks that did not cover the noisy condition. (2) “A de-tuned prior gives the learned term room” was predicted and is false: at 25 % gains MAACC is worse than PID on every weather and both loops (−6 % to −35 %). A weaker prior leaves a larger error for the actor to chase, and chasing it with an incremental policy is harder than leaving it alone.

Read the clean condition by its absolute numbers. MAACC looks 45–69 % worse on nitrate, but PID's IAE there is about 0.002 against MAACC's 0.003. Both are negligible next to the 0.06–0.13 of the noisy condition. With no disturbance to reject there is nothing to learn, and any exploration is pure cost.

8. Our tracking-layer extension (PANDA): a negative result

u = b + g·a + f: the MAACC agents also see a 6-number influent forecast, the learned term is scaled by an uncertainty gate g, and an anticipatory feed-forward f is solved by differentiating a neural digital twin over a 48-min forecast horizon (details on the prediction models page). Setting g = 1 and f = 0 gives back eq. (7).

PANDA is worse than the PID prior on dissolved oxygen in every condition (−4 % to −32 % under noise and de-tuning, and worse still in the clean setting), and that is the loop it was designed for. On nitrate it is better under noise (+6 % to +16 %) and worse elsewhere. The negative result is kept and written up in docs/06_tracking_layer_feedforward.md. The prediction effort then moved to paper 2, where the objective is energy rather than set-point tracking.

10. What is not reproduced

  • The paper's Table II numbers. BSM1 is re-implemented from its published definition, and the influent is statistically equivalent rather than the official files. Absolute IAE/ISE/DEVmax values are not comparable.
  • The seven comparison methods (RSCMPC, MADRL-PID, SUP-HDP, dHDP, DPPGADP, ACD-VarInf, FAACC) are not implemented. Only the PID prior and MAACC are compared.
  • The “> 90 % training success over 20 runs” statistic was not measured. Two seeds per configuration were run in the committed benchmark.
  • The paper's own weight matrices W, Y, R, P and network sizes are not all stated, so the values here were chosen by us (see §8).
