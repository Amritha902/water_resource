The prediction models

Both papers' controllers only react: every signal they see is a consequence of a disturbance that has already reached the plant. The influent is the main disturbance, and it is measured at the inlet and largely predictable. These models predict it, and each one is re-checked here on data it never saw, against a simple baseline.

1. Where they are used

Influent forecaster

Dilated causal TCN (6 blocks, dilations 1–32, width 64) over 24 h of inlet flow, NH₄ and readily biodegradable COD at 15 min. It outputs 10/50/90 % quantiles for the next 2 h (monotone by cumulative softplus, pinball loss) plus a dry/rain/storm head. It feeds PANDA (paper 1), PANDA-RL (paper 2) and the early warning. src/wwtp/forecast/

Digital twin (paper 1)

GRU (hidden 96) on a 6-min grid: h_t = GRU([s_t, u_t, d_t], h_{t−1}), s_{t+1} = s_t + g(h_t), plus an effluent-NH₄ head. It is differentiable in u, so PANDA's feed-forward can ask “what if I move now?”. It was trained on 60 % open-loop PRBS and 40 % dithered-PID data with scheduled sampling. Paper 2 uses a separate aeration twin for Dyna. src/wwtp/twin/

Effluent-ammonium early warning

A causal TCN with a quantile head and a discrete-time hazard head. It predicts whether, and how far, effluent NH₄ will break 4 mg/L in the next 12–120 min, using only signals a plant already measures. It is a stand-alone decision aid for the operator. src/wwtp/warning/

2. Influent forecaster

Checked on the current code and influent generator. The canonical profiles were never used in training, and the 24 “fresh” scenarios use seeds that were never used in training either. The forecast for each moment is the one issued 2 hours earlier, plotted at the time it refers to.

Weather

The regime classifier does not generalise

The reported validation accuracy (95.8 % in artifacts/forecaster_history.json, quoted as 95 % in the docs) came from a validation split drawn at random from overlapping windows of the same scenarios, which leaks. On fresh scenarios it is near chance (below). The label is also ill-posed: each window carries its scenario's weather, but on the calm days of a “storm” scenario nothing in 24 h of history can show a storm is coming. The fix is a per-window label (“is a wet-weather event in this window?”). Until then the P(wet) signal PANDA receives is close to noise.

3. Neural digital twin

Two checks, both on data the twin never saw. Step tests: the real plant and the twin, settled under constant dry-average influent, receive the same actuator step. Free-running prediction: from a fresh rollout, the twin is warmed on 1.6 h of history and then predicts 4.8 h ahead on its own outputs, given the real actuator moves and influent.

Closed-loop identification bias, and why the PRBS data is required. The first twin was trained only on PID-controlled data. With the PID in the loop, KLa₅ rises exactly when oxygen demand rises, so in that data more air does not visibly mean more oxygen. The twin learned a near-zero aeration gain (40 → 220 1/d moved DO by 0.05 g/m³), and differentiating it moved the actuator the wrong way. Refitting with 60 % open-loop pseudo-random excitation fixed the signs, as the step tests above show. tests/test_learned_models.py::test_trained_twin_has_the_right_control_gains

4. Effluent-ammonium early warning

The question, every 6 minutes: will effluent NH₄-N break the 4 g/m³ discharge limit within 12, 30, 60, 90 or 120 min, and how high will it go? Inputs: only signals a plant already measures online, namely inlet flow, NH₄ and COD; DO₅ and NO₂ probes; the controller's own KLa₅ and Qₐ; the effluent NH₄ analyser (noisy, and stale between 12-min cycles); water temperature; time of day; and the forecaster's 6-number outlook. Why the plants vary: at the paper's settings effluent NH₄ never exceeds 2.4 g/m³, so there is nothing to warn about. Real plants trim aeration to save energy and lose nitrification in cold water. Each simulated plant therefore draws aeration in reactors 3–4 from 160–240 1/d, temperature from 11.5–17 °C (BSM2 Arrhenius factors on the nitrifier growth and decay rates) and the DO set point from 0.8–2.0. Train, validation and test plants are disjoint: 96, 16 and 24 plants of 10 days each.

Model

Causal TCN (dilations 1–16, width 64) over 6 h of the 17 signals. Level head: 10/50/90 % quantiles of effluent NH₄ at each horizon. Hazard head: a discrete-time survival model. For each horizon interval it predicts the probability λ_j that the first exceedance happens there, and P(violation within h_j) = 1 − ∏(1 − λ_l). The warning probability therefore cannot decrease as the horizon grows. src/wwtp/warning/

How it is scored

Against today's practice (alarm when the analyser reads above the limit), trend extrapolation of the analyser, and gradient boosting on hand-made features of the same signals. The main scores use only moments when the plant is still compliant, because on an ongoing violation copying the analyser is trivially right. Operationally, each method's alarm threshold is set on validation plants to allow at most 1 false alarm per compliant day, and lead time is measured on test plants.
